An Introduction to Competency-Based Learning

A short introduction to competency-based learning, written for anyone curious about how it might make a learning team more effective.

What is competency-based learning?

Competency-based learning (CBL) is a method of instruction and evaluation built on learners demonstrating their mastery of a subject. It focuses on having learners show what they know, applying the concepts they have learned to evaluations that prove they have genuinely grasped the material.

📕

Competency-based learning is sometimes called mastery-based learning or proficiency-based learning. The terms are interchangeable.

An example competency

Here is a simple example of what a competency might look like:

🏆

Problem solving. Uses rigorous logic and methods to solve difficult problems with effective solutions; probes all fruitful sources for answers; can see hidden problems; is excellent at honest analysis; looks beyond the obvious and doesn't stop at the first answers.

Why a fast-moving business needs it

Training teams create materials for a vast body of knowledge, and most of that knowledge is experienced as a course or as instructor-led training. In a fast-moving business, training topics change rapidly. Ad policy and product training might have several updates a month. That often means whole courses become invalid, which erodes the trust of learners. In a better system, we would be able to respond to changes in the material quickly.

To do that, we need to create smaller chunks of knowledge that can be tracked and managed. That would let us better manage the relationship between stakeholders, subject matter experts and learning teams. CBL is inherently granular, and it could support a practice that is both dynamic and responsive.

How is it different from other approaches?

Competency-based learning differs from traditional learning methods in several ways.

  1. Focus on outcomes rather than time. The focus is on what learners can do rather than how long they spend learning. This emphasizes mastery over duration, and it may mean deemphasizing KPIs built around increasing learning time.
  2. Assessment of mastery rather than completion. Assessments measure whether learners have mastered specific competencies, not whether they finished a course or program.
  3. Personalized learning pathways. CBL allows for flexible, adaptive and individualized pathways that account for each learner's strengths and interests.
  4. Prior learning and credit. CBL recognizes prior learning and credits people for skills they already hold, which reduces redundant learning. This usually requires more rigorous pre-assessment to establish a baseline.
  5. Emphasis on demonstration of skills. At its best, learners demonstrate understanding through real-world applications, projects or performances rather than through written tests and memorization alone.

Why is it valuable?

By adopting competency-based learning, a business can build a more agile and responsive workforce.

  1. Continuous improvement. Regularly updating competencies and training programs embeds a culture of continuous learning.
  2. Cost savings. Targeted training that addresses specific skill gaps reduces the costs tied to turnover, recruitment and retraining.
  3. Performance improvement. Competencies make performance gaps visible, so training can be aimed at the gap rather than at everyone.
  4. Job relevance. Training aligned to the actual needs of a role wastes less of the learner's time and earns more of their attention.
