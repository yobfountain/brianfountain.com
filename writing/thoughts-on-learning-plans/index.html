LEARNING ARCHITECTURE

Thoughts on Learning Plans

Notes on the architectural direction of a learning platform, and on the problem of modeling a learning journey that is different for every single employee.

Knowledge granularity

Ultimately it is desirable to have the smallest possible chunk of knowledge. Eventually that chunk is the competency. At the moment the most reasonable chunk is the course: in early versions of the platform we track a learner's journey by the courses they complete.

Responsibility emitters

An individual employee's responsibilities are a composite of several contexts that are probably unique to them. The role of business development manager brings an expectation of certain knowledge and skills, and most people in that role need the same knowledge. But two of them might have additional contexts that change their particular learning journey, such as the industry they specialize in: automotive against consumer packaged goods.

Here is a list of the contexts that might emit additional responsibilities.

  • Role. Presumably your role contributes the most to what is expected of you.
  • Contributor type. Most organizations have additional expectations of people who lead.
    • Individual contributor
    • Lead
    • Director
  • Area of focus or region. Does the role carry a specialization that requires additional training? Regulated industries, for example.
  • Location. A less important context, but perhaps a more actionable one for onboarding. How to get a new employee badge, for instance.
  • Employment type. Is this person a full-time employee or a third-party contractor?
  • Organization. Does the learner work for the company or outside it? An account manager at an ad agency, for example.
  • Individual. Ultimately each person also works with their leader to plot their own path through their career.

Modeling the journey

These unique journeys have to be modeled in a database in such a way that each context can be updated dynamically without sacrificing what is bespoke about an individual employee. The proposal is a two-phase approach: start with something simpler, then migrate to a model that affords greater complexity.
