Putting Competency-Based Learning into Practice

How a business puts competency-based learning into everyday practice, once competencies are arranged into a framework rather than kept as a list.

Overview

When an ecosystem of competencies is arranged into a meaningful framework, usually centered on roles, it can have a multiplier effect across the business. Three areas benefit most.

🏆

KSAs and competencies. Knowledge, skills and abilities (KSAs) is a broad term for the information, skills and abilities needed to perform a specific job or task. KSAs are often used interchangeably with competencies.

Competency-based recruiting

Competency-based recruitment evaluates candidates on the skills, knowledge and abilities essential to a specific role. It emphasizes objective criteria over subjective factors like years of experience or educational qualifications, and it analyzes a candidate's behavioral characteristics and past experience to see how well they demonstrate the competencies the role actually requires.

The process usually runs in the same order: define the competencies, rank them, choose selection methods that measure potential and future performance, run semi-structured interviews, and collect references.

Role-based learning journeys

If we took any role in the business and followed someone in it for a few weeks, writing down their actions, we would more or less have a model of that role. Once we know which competencies are required to excel in it, training teams can design learning experiences that target those specific skills.

The same knowledge works in the other direction. When people can see what is required to excel in a role, they can take a proactive approach to preparing for the next step in their career rather than waiting to be told.

Performance evaluation

Competencies provide clear criteria for evaluating performance, which makes evaluations more consistent and harder to bias. A standardized set lets evaluators compare performance across different roles or departments, giving a more accurate picture of individual strengths and weaknesses, and it creates the opening for development plans aimed at specific gaps.

Conclusion

Each of these three areas provides clarity and consistency on its own. Combined into a single approach they produce considerably more, but getting there takes a unified model and careful execution.
