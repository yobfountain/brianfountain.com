INSTRUCTIONAL DESIGN

Teach the model, measure the mastery, let the specifics regenerate

I have designed learning for career changers writing their first line of Ruby, for teams at Disney and Estée Lauder, for Google's advertisers, and for a global sales organization whose product changed every month. The audiences differ, and the method does not: find the mental model underneath the skill, teach that, assess whether it stuck, and put the content where the learner already is.

TikTok, Global Business Solutions2021–2025

Competency-based learning for a sales organization that never stopped changing

Global Business Solutions is TikTok's sales organization: account managers, sellers, and the ops leads who supported them, across every region, selling an ad product suite that changed several times a month. Each change invalidated whole courses. My job was to lead the department's effort to scale technical learning across that organization, and the first thing I learned was that the course was the wrong unit.

I introduced and architected a competency-based practice, starting with the internal papers that made the case, then the data model, then the measurement. The papers argued for granularity: break knowledge into small, individually trackable chunks that can be updated surgically and retired one at a time, so a product change costs a paragraph instead of a course. We started at course-level granularity, a two-phase compromise between the model I wanted and what the data could yet support. On top of that model sat a competency management system that assembled a personalized learning journey for each person from their region, sales vertical and client size, with scoped reports so leaders could see where their teams stood. I designed; a partner facilitator team delivered.

25%less facilitator-led training for some teams once journeys replaced how they consumed training
Level 4Kirkpatrick measurement, built with a data scientist, mapping training consumption to sales outcomes
Live dataa custom dashboard integrated with the LMS so leaders read their teams' progress in real time

The measurement mattered as much as the content. Rolling quarterly cohort analysis tied what people consumed to what they sold, and competency lift gave us a direct capability metric. It was also the instrument that could prove me wrong, which is the only kind worth building. The arguments from those years are adapted for a general reader in three pieces on competency-based learning.

Journey, an internal pilot
TikTok, Global Business Solutionsprototype

Training delivered where the learner already spends the day

The people we were training spent their days inside a vertical feed, so Journey put the training there too. Every lesson ran under a minute and taught exactly one thing. The knowledge check came on the very next swipe, while the idea was still warm. The scenarios were rewritten for each role, so an account manager and an ops lead learned the same skill in different clothes. A third swipe offered an optional weekly stitch competition, which turned practice into something people wanted to be seen doing, and a board of stickers gave a half-finished week a reason to be finished.

I rebuilt the pilot as a working demo: the watch-and-check loop, the optional stitch, and the journey board, on a phone you can swipe.

Try the Journey demo

Journey is the clearest expression of the design rule I keep coming back to: the format should cost the learner nothing. Nobody had to leave their day, translate a generic example into their own work, or remember to return. The featured-entry stage did more for participation than any reminder email.

Thinkful2020

A free on-ramp for people deciding whether they can code

Thinkful's Prep program is the free course that readies prospective students for the qualifying assessment, which makes it the first thing a nervous career changer meets. I was the creative lead on its redesign across HTML, CSS and JavaScript, shaping the voice and the approach. The design problem was confidence as much as syntax: the learner has to leave believing they belong in the next course, and the only honest way to give them that belief is to have them build something real early and often.

New York Code and Design Academy2014–2017

Writing the standard down so instructors I never met could deliver it

NYCDA grew from three locations to seven while I was there, and a school cannot scale on the instructors who happen to be in the building. I authored the mission, the values, the teacher-training process and the curriculum-design process, which is how people I never met delivered the program to our standard during the expansion. I also led the immersive bootcamp as Lead Instructor, where fluency was the product being sold and graduates' employment was the measure.

On the corporate side I built the training product line from scratch and led its design and delivery for Fortune 500 clients, including Walt Disney Parks and Resorts Technology Group and Estée Lauder. Corporate training is instructional design with a harder deadline: the room is full of experts in something else, the time is paid for by the hour, and the outcome has to show up in their work the following week.
